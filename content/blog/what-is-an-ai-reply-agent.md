An AI reply agent reads incoming messages, works out what the person wants, and either replies straight away or prepares a draft for your team to approve. It is the written-message cousin of an AI receptionist.

## Where it helps

- **Shared email inboxes** such as info@ and support@, which fill up with the same questions.
- **WhatsApp**, where customers expect an answer in minutes.
- **Instagram and Facebook messages**, which are easy to miss entirely.
- **Google reviews**, where a thoughtful, timely reply affects what future customers think.

## How it works

1. A message arrives.
2. The agent reads it and identifies the topic, the urgency and, where possible, the customer.
3. It looks up what it needs: an order, a policy, your help content, availability.
4. It writes a reply in your tone of voice.
5. Depending on your rules, the reply is sent automatically or waits for a person to approve it.

## Draft first, automate later

The safest way to start is **approval mode**: every reply is drafted and a person checks it before it is sent. After a few weeks you will see which kinds of message the agent handles reliably. Those can go out automatically, while everything else still waits for a person.

Some messages should always go to a person: complaints, refunds, anything legal or medical, and negative reviews.

## What makes a good reply agent

- **It answers from your information, not guesswork.** If it does not know, it says so and passes the message on.
- **It sounds like you.** It is set up with your tone, your standard answers and examples of good past replies.
- **It shows its reasoning.** When a message is held for a person, the reason is visible.
- **It is measured.** Track reply time, how many replies needed editing, and how many were escalated.

## What it should not do

It should not invent prices, promises or policies, reply to sensitive complaints without a person, or pretend to be a named member of staff.
